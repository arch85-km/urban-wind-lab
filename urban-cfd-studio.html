Method
Every figure in this panel comes from a flow solve running in this browser tab. Nothing is precomputed or looked up.
Flow solver
A D3Q19 lattice‑Boltzmann scheme: 19 discrete velocities per cell, advanced by streaming and a BGK collision, with a Smagorinsky sub‑grid model supplying the turbulent viscosity (large‑eddy style closure).
fq(x+eq, t+1) = fq(x,t) − ω [ fq − fqeq ]
fqeq = wq ρ [ 1 + 3(eq·u) + 4.5(eq·u)² − 1.5|u|² ]
τ = ½( τ₀ + √( τ₀² + 18√2 C² |Π| / ρ ) )
Π is the second moment of the non‑equilibrium distribution, so the eddy viscosity rises exactly where the flow is being sheared.
Boundary conditions
Upwind facesABL log‑law inlet
U(z) = U₁₀ · ln((z+z₀)/z₀) / ln((10+z₀)/z₀)
Downwind facesZero gradient
Domain topDriven free stream
Ground & buildingsHalf‑way bounce‑back
The inlet is applied to whichever faces face upwind, so any wind azimuth is solved without rebuilding the model.
Geometry
Triangles are voxelised onto the lattice by a surface pass plus a parity fill, producing the solid mask the bounce‑back uses. Anything finer than one cell is not resolved.
Reported quantities
|U| = √(u² + v² + w²)   amplification = |U| / U₁₀
Cp = (p − pref) / (½ρU₁₀²) = 2(ρlat−1) / (3 uref²)
Comfort classes apply the Lawson LDDC thresholds to the solved local speed on the 1.5 m plane.
Scaling
The solve runs in lattice units at a fixed reference velocity and the field is stored normalised, as U/U₁₀ and Cp. Both are Reynolds‑independent for sharp‑edged bluff bodies, which is why changing the wind speed rescales the result instantly while changing direction or roughness starts a new solve.
Live parameters
Not modelled. Isothermal — no buoyancy, stack effect or solar heating. No surrounding terrain, vegetation or porosity. Turbulence is a sub‑grid model on a coarse lattice, not a resolved LES or a validated RANS closure. A certified comfort assessment uses gust‑equivalent speeds weighted by the annual frequency of every wind direction; this shows one direction at a time.
